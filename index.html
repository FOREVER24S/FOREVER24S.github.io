I am a Principal R&D Engineer at BYD in Shenzhen, working on the high-voltage distribution box inside EV battery packs: how power is routed, switched and protected, and what the design has to do when something goes wrong. Before this I spent five years on a Ph.D. in materials science, which is a useful way to arrive at power hardware - it trains you to think about the worst case first.

The doctorate (Peking University, 2026) was on biodegradable metals and neurovascular devices: alloy design, wire processing, corrosion and fatigue, additive manufacturing, then devices tested in animals and - for one retriever design - in 80 clinical cases. Nineteen papers and five P.R. China patents came out of it. The subject changed; the question did not. What fails first, and how would you know?

What I want next is embodied intelligence: perception, manipulation and the policy side of machines that act in the physical world. Coming from power hardware, my interest is in the physical layer of that - actuators, sensing, and the failure modes you only meet on a real machine.

Research

Four lines of work, and one habit underneath them: find out what breaks before a customer does. High-voltage power distribution and battery systems are the day job; embodied intelligence is where I am heading; materials and reliability are the training that connects them.

High-voltage power distribution

Contactors, fuses, pre-charge and current sensing inside an EV battery pack - and inside the charging pile that feeds it. Power routing, switching and protection, including the high-power path flash charging needs, and the behaviour the design owes under fault.

Batteries across machines

The same cell chemistry meeting three very different duty cycles: electric vehicles, low-altitude aircraft and humanoid robots. Different safety envelopes, mass budgets, load profiles and charging rates - five minutes to 70% is one end of that range, and a walking machine is the other. I am writing a review of the comparison.

Embodied intelligence

Perception, manipulation and policy for machines that act in the physical world. My interest sits in the physical layer: actuators, sensing, and the failure modes that only appear on real hardware. Half of it is a data problem, and the other half is a machine that has to survive contact.

Materials & reliability

Where I started, and still how I read a design: alloy design, corrosion and fatigue, manufacturing processes, and testing to destruction. A doctorate's worth of practice in assuming the specification is optimistic.

The through-line is the same question at every scale: what has to hold when the conditions get worse than the specification.
